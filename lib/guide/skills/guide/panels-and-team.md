# Panels and the team

Every panel on the page is one Claude Code session, called a seat. There is one Leader and any number of Workers.

A panel shows a turn's answer, not its way there. Words a session writes between its calls are held back while the turn runs: they are shown at its end only when the turn said nothing else, and not at all when it ended with nothing for the panel. The first words after a line typed while the turn runs are shown at once. Until the end, the panel shows the call the session has out.

## The Leader
- The session the person talks to. It hires, delegates, relays, and settles what the instance may do. It does no project work itself: a check, a clone, a build or a test is a hire.
- Its panel is always there. The next thing typed to it starts it.
- Only the Leader can hire, stop, restart and let go of Workers, add permission rules, and park the room. All of it is asked for in words: there are no buttons for any of it. The page's controls are few: a stop button over the box on every panel while its turn is running, which interrupts that turn (on a computer with a mouse it shows while the mouse moves, for 2 seconds after it stops, and while the mouse is on it; on a touch screen it is always there), and on the Leader's head a light/dark toggle, two notification switches (action needed, end of turn), a switch that shows or hides the messages between the Leader and the Workers, a switch that shows or hides the Leader's status, the two auto-mode switches below, and the reload and restart buttons (see "Restart, update and reload from the page").
- The Leader's head also carries the account's usage line: the 5-hour window and the time to its reset, then "all" (the weekly window over every model), then "fable" (Fable's own weekly window), each with its reset, e.g. "8% (3h) · all 86% (6d) · fable 20% (6d)". An account whose usage lists no Fable window has no "fable" part. A narrow window shows the percentages alone, "8% / 86% / 20%"; the tooltip says when each window resets and when the reading was taken.

## The Leader's status
- Right above the Leader's box, below its "Waiting for instructions" line, one row headed "Status" says what waits for the person and the loose ends: what was started and stopped, or found and not closed, that nobody is on. It is the file `desks/<Leader>/STATUS.md`, which the Leader keeps, shown as it stood when the Leader last confirmed it; the time on the right is that confirmation. An edit the Leader has not confirmed does not show.
- The Leader confirms it at the end of every turn that brought it anything (a line from the person, a Worker's message, an event from the server), editing it first when it no longer holds. A message on the panel says what happened; the status says only what waits.
- The words beside the time: "up to date ✓" when nothing has reached the Leader since; "checking…" while the Leader is on something new; "3 new since" (in the warning colour) when a turn ended without a confirmation; "Leader stopped" while the Leader has no session or the server is gone. The row stays shown either way.
- A click on its time asks the Leader to check the status; it does not quote the row into the box as a click on a message's time does. The Leader confirms, with or without an edit, and says nothing on the panel. A click while the Leader has no session starts it, as any message does.
- A click on one of its bullets puts `(status/HH:MM:SS/N)` into the box at the cursor: the status's time and the bullet's number. When the line is sent, the page writes it out as one line, `> Status (HH:MM:SS) - ` and the bullet's text as it was shown, on a line of its own, so the Leader reads the bullet the person clicked even if the status has changed since. Only the page that was clicked knows the bullet: after a reload the token goes as typed. In the person's row, such a line is drawn as a quote.
- The row appears after the Leader's first confirmation and is as tall as its text, so the Leader keeps it short: one bulleted list, one thing per bullet, what waits for the person first, each starting with a bold "Waiting for you:", then the loose ends, in the same order from one confirmation to the next. A result the Leader has reported is a "Waiting for you:" bullet until the person closes its topic; work that is moving is not in it, and a bullet goes the moment it no longer waits or is no longer loose. A ticket or a page is a link with a short label. No title or headings. With nothing waiting and nothing loose, one bullet says so. A switch on the Leader's head hides it in this browser. Kept on disk, it is the same after a restart of the server.

## Auto mode
- In Claude Code's auto mode a classifier settles the calls no rule settles, instead of a permission popup. Every session starts in it, as the Claude Code CLI does. Two switches on the Leader's head, both bolt icons, turn it off (Manual, where every such call asks on a popup): the first for the Leader's own session, the second for all the Workers' sessions.
- The switches belong to the server, not to the browser: every open page shows the same state, and both are on whenever the server starts. Nothing about them is saved, so a switch turned off lasts until the server stops.
- A click reaches the running sessions of that role at once, with no restart, and sessions started later start in that mode. A model that has no auto mode keeps asking with popups, without saying so. Haiku has auto mode, like the other models.
- A call the classifier refuses raises no popup. The session is told the call failed, and its panel gets a row, "Blocked by auto mode: <category> — <the call>", with the classifier's category and the call, cut when it is very long. A call the classifier could not judge is not a refusal: its row says "Not run — auto mode's check gave no verdict — <the call>", the session follows what Claude Code tells it about trying again, and says so if the call still did not run. A call a deny rule refuses gets no such row.

## Restart, update and reload from the page
- The restart button, a power icon last on the Leader's head, does what `ovai restart` does. It asks first, because the room parks as at `ovai stop`: every Worker is closed, the Leader writes its desk, and nothing starts the Workers again. While a newer release is out, the version on the Leader's head is red, and a click on it does `ovai stop`, `ovai update` and `ovai start`, after the same question, which also names the version that is out and its release date. The start runs even when the update failed. The page reloads itself on the new version. While the room is parking, a press on either does nothing: press again once the park is over.
- Neither button is there on an instance installed with port 0. Such an instance comes back on another port, where the open page cannot follow. Use `ovai restart` there, then the link it prints.
- While the server is away, the Leader's bottom line says "Restarting…" or "Updating…". If no server answers after the time the commands themselves wait before giving up (65 seconds with the default park settings), the line names `<instance>/runtime.log`, where the commands' output is appended, and says that `ovai start` starts the server.
- The reload button, a circular arrow beside the restart button, asks every running session to read its skills and then its plugins again, as `/reload-skills` and `/reload-plugins` would at a terminal, with no restart. Use it after installing a plugin or adding a skill in admin mode. Each panel gets one row, and a press while a reload is under way does nothing. "Skills and plugins reloaded" means both were applied. When reloading the plugins would change the tools that the conversation's cache rests on, they are held instead: the row says so, and the plugins load at that session's next start. Plugin errors are counted in the row and listed by `/plugin` in admin mode. Settings, MCP servers and `customization/` are still read only at a session's start.

## Workers
- A Worker has one task at a time, keeps its desk saying where it stands, and reports to the Leader when done.
- Ask the Leader for help and it hires. A new Worker gets the next free name from a fixed list of thirty (Paul, Jane, Jack, …). The name cannot be chosen.
- A hire is refused while the usage window has reached its first threshold, or while the server is stopping.
- Stopping a Worker: it is told it is closing, writes its desk, and goes when its turn is over. Restarting: the same, then a new session starts from the desk. `hire` on a stopped Worker's name brings it back on the same desk, with its panel log kept.
- Letting a Worker go (retire): its desk is moved whole to `archive/`, its panel leaves the page, and its name is free again. Only a stopped Worker can be retired. The Leader retires a stopped Worker once nothing open names it — no topic of the person's that is still open, no line in its status — and once `knowledge/` holds what its desk knew.
- With every name on the list held by a desk, a new Worker gets a made-up name, Dev and three digits, and the Leader is told how many desks are stopped.
- A topic is something the person asked for. It stays open, on the Leader's desk and in its status, until the person says it is done, accepts it, uses the result or drops it: a report closes nothing.

## Models and effort
- The Leader's and the Workers' default models are in `openovai.json`, set at install. To change one, ask the Leader ("Workers on sonnet by default"); it writes the file (see `setup-and-update.md`). A change is taken while the server runs, with no restart: the new Workers' default reaches every Worker without a model of its own at its next start, and the Leader's at the Leader's next start (see `setup-and-update.md`).
- Ask the Leader to hire a Worker on another model, e.g. "sonnet" or "opus/low". The part after the slash is the reasoning effort (low, medium, high, xhigh, max). With none, Claude Code picks its own. The choice is kept in `desks/<Name>/MODEL`, and a later hire on that desk with a model replaces it.
- A model written as an alias (`opus`, `opus/high`, `sonnet`) runs the newest model of that name that the pinned Claude Code ships, so a newer model arrives with a Claude Code update and nothing in `openovai.json` or on a desk changes. A full id such as `claude-opus-5-5` pins that one version: use it only for a task that needs exactly that model. This holds for the default models and for a hire alike.
- A running session keeps the model it started on. It changes at its next start.
- Fast mode (Claude Code's `/fast`: Opus answers faster at a higher price, billed from the account's usage credits) is off for the Leader and the Workers, whatever the person's own Claude Code setting says. It is off because ovai starts sessions on demand: at the command line fast mode applies to the one session in front of the person, but here ten Workers on fast mode could run up a large bill within minutes. It may come back later as a choice per Worker, beside its model and effort.

## Desks
`desks/<Name>/` is one seat's directory:
- `STATE.md` is the desk file. Its first line is a title and a status everyone sees; below that is what the session keeps: what is true now, not how it got there. A new session on that desk starts from it: the conversation of the one before is gone.
- `STATE-<time>.md` is a copy of the whole desk, saved by the server when the desk grows past the desk limit, 128 KB unless `openovai.json` sets `"desk": { "limit": <bytes> }`. To change it, ask the Leader ("raise the desk limit to 256 KB"; see `setup-and-update.md`). A new limit applies from the next desk write, with no restart. The session that owns the desk is told once per crossing and asked to archive what current work no longer needs, or to have the limit raised when most of it is still open. The copies are kept beside the desk and go with it when it is retired.
- `conversation.json` is the panel, kept across restarts.
- A seat's history is its panel, the copies of its desk and the git log of each project it worked in. Ask a seat about the past and it searches them.
- The Leader's directory also holds `STATUS.md`, its status for the person, and `status.json`, the status as last confirmed and how much has reached the Leader since: what the page shows.
- `persona.md` is what the session was told at its start.

## Why a session stopped or went away
- Idle: a Worker nobody has spoken to is closed after 55 minutes idle, with the Leader told at 10 and 50. The Leader is asked to stop at 55 and ended at 60. A session working a long turn is not idle. The next message to the Leader starts it again; a Worker comes back with `hire` on its name. The minutes are settings: ask the Leader to change them (see `setup-and-update.md`).
- Usage window: at the first threshold (90% of the 5-hour window, 97% of the weekly one), hiring is refused. At the second (95%, 99%), Workers on that model are closed and turns are held until the window resets. When a window that reached a threshold resets, the server starts the Leader again, and the Leader's bottom line says so until then (below). The thresholds are settings: ask the Leader to change them.
- Stopped or restarted by the Leader, or the room parked for the day.
- `ovai stop` parks the room: every Worker is closed, the Leader writes its desk, then the server stops. Nothing restarts Workers after `ovai start`. Desks and panels stay on disk; ask the Leader to bring back who is needed.
- A Worker's panel dims when its process ends, and is removed about 30 seconds later. The Leader's panel stays: while it has no process its dot is grey and its bottom line says "<Name> has left"; the next message starts a fresh session.

## The Leader's bottom line
- On a turn it says what the Leader is doing. Off a turn it is the state, then each fact that holds, in this order, joined by " · ": "Waiting for instructions" or "<Name> has left"; then "Wake-up: 18:40" or "Quota reset: 18:40", whichever comes first; then "Runs: Axel, Mia", the seats with a long run going (three names, then "and 2 more").
- A time is the time alone today, the full weekday within the week ("Friday 09:00"), the date further on ("2026-11-25 09:00").
- Hovering the line says each part in full, one line each: that the Leader is not running and the next message starts a fresh session; the wake-up's reason, or the quota window that resets; each run going, with its seat and how long it has run.
- The wake-up is set by the Leader: by itself when work waits on a time, such as a usage window's reset, or when asked ("pick this up at 9:30"); a quota reset after a window was spent starts it too. A message starts the Leader sooner, and the wake-up still comes at its time. Parking the room, or a server stop, cancels it.

## Long runs
- Work that may outlast a session — an overnight sweep, a batch over hundreds of files, a long build — is a long run. A Worker starts it in its shell with `<instance>/bin/ovai run <name> -- <command>`. A background command a session starts any other way ends with that session, and Claude Code may stop it sooner.
- A run survives its Worker's stop, restart, idle close and quota stop, and a server stop, restart or update. It does not survive a reboot: it is then told as lost, and started again under the same name it resumes, when its command skips what it already saved.
- When it ends, the Worker that started it is told; when that Worker is not running, the Leader is, and decides whether to bring the Worker back or tell you. While the room is parked nobody is started for it: the notice waits for the Leader's next start. A room stays parked until the server stops; after a server start or restart, a run that ends starts the Leader. To have the Leader up when a run should finish, ask it to park with a wake-up set for that time.
- Each run is a directory on its Worker's desk, `desks/<Name>/runs/<run>/`, with its command, its log and how it ended; it goes to `archive/` with the desk. The Leader cannot retire a Worker with a run going, or with a run whose end it has not been told yet.
- `<instance>/bin/ovai runs` lists them from a terminal, `ovai run --stop <Name>/<run>` stops one. A seat has at most 8 runs going and a run is stopped after 24 hours; both are settings (`runs.most`, `runs.hours`): ask the Leader to change them.

## When Claude's service cuts a reply
- Sometimes Claude's service fails in the middle of a session's turn, for example with "API Error: 403", "API Error: 529 Overloaded" or "Request timed out". The session does not answer then, and the panel shows a row of ovai's own, on a dashed ground, in place of a reply.
- Claude Code tries again by itself first when the service has a server error, is overloaded, loses the connection or does not answer in time: up to 10 times, waiting longer each time, so about three minutes go by before the row appears. A refused request (403) is not tried again.
- While Claude Code tries again, the Leader's bottom line reads "Claude's service failed, trying again (3 of 10)", with the try it is on. A Worker's head reads "retrying (3 of 10)" instead of "working", and the same sentence shows on hover. As soon as the session moves on, the line and the head go back to what they said before.
- The row says whether the session got the message, and what it did before the failure: each action with its time, as the bottom line names it. When nothing was done, it says so.
- The session keeps the message and the results of what it did. Send "go on" and it replies; nothing needs repeating, and nothing it did is done twice. ovai does not send it by itself.
- The row ends with "Source:" and Claude Code's own words for the failure, then a link to https://status.claude.com/ to check whether the service is down.
- When a session was working on its own, with no message in the turn, the row says so; send "go on" and it carries on.
- A session whose very first turn fails is ended instead, and so is one that was being stopped or restarted when the failure came: the row says it stopped and to send the message again, since the next message starts a fresh session that never got it. An expired sign-in has a row of its own, which says to run `ovai login`.

## Typing to a Worker
Typing on a Worker's panel speaks to that Worker directly. It answers, and the Leader is told what was said. A panel whose process is gone takes no typing.

## Previous messages with the up arrow
- Each input box keeps the last 10 messages typed on that panel, read from the panel itself, so they survive a page reload and differ from panel to panel. A message sent twice in a row counts once.
- Up moves the cursor up as usual. Once the cursor is at the very start of the box, the next Up shows the previous message, greyed and with no cursor. What was being typed is set aside, not lost.
- While a previous message shows, Up goes further back and Down comes forward. Down past the newest brings back what was being typed, and so does Escape. Other keys do nothing.
- Enter, or a click into the box, puts the message shown into the box to edit. It is not sent. A second Enter sends it.
- This is for a keyboard. Phones and tablets are not supported.
